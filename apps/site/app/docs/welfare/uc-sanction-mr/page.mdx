# Universal Credit sanction

Draft. Not counsel-reviewed. ChatLegal is not a solicitor. This is general information about a Universal Credit sanction for someone in England (`EW`), not legal advice, and not representation. Say if you live in Scotland or Northern Ireland. Some support differs, and this page does not mix those rules in.

## Plain-English scope

A sanction is a Department for Work and Pensions decision that reduces Universal Credit. This page describes two public routes: a recoverable hardship payment if the reduced award does not cover essentials, and mandatory reconsideration if the decision itself is disputed. It does not say whether the Department will change a decision.

## If there is no money for essentials

GOV.UK says that if Universal Credit is reduced by a sanction, a person may be able to get a recoverable hardship payment for food, heating, or hygiene. It is paid back from future Universal Credit. GOV.UK lists conditions. The gold note records two of them: a person can apply only once a reduced payment has been made, and they must have met their work-related requirements in the 7 days before they apply. Ask through the Universal Credit journal, a work coach, or the Universal Credit helpline.

**[verify] Hardship eligibility.** GOV.UK lists further conditions (including the size of the sanction). The gold note paraphrases only part of that list. This page does not complete it.

If there is no money for food, heating, or rent, do not wait on this page. Contact the local council or a local advice service today. Emergency food, as a link only: [Trussell](https://www.trussell.org.uk/emergency-food).

## Mandatory reconsideration

GOV.UK says that if you disagree with a benefit decision, including Universal Credit, you can ask for it to be looked at again. That is mandatory reconsideration. You usually need to ask within one month. For Universal Credit that can be done in the journal. A late request needs a good reason, and GOV.UK says you should explain why it is late.

**[verify] When the month starts.** GOV.UK says both "the date of the decision" and "the date on your decision letter". For a sanction that arrives as a journal message, counsel has not confirmed that the journal date is the date that starts the month. Check the date on the decision and act now rather than near the end. This page does not count the days.

When you ask, GOV.UK says you explain what part of the decision is wrong and why, and you can send evidence you have not sent before. GOV.UK says that if you could not meet a commitment because you were unexpectedly ill or had a hospital appointment, and the Department decides you had a good reason, your payment will not be reduced. The Department looks at the whole claim again. GOV.UK says payments can go up or down. This page will not predict which.

You then get a mandatory reconsideration notice. If you still disagree, GOV.UK says you can appeal to the Social Security and Child Support Tribunal, usually within one month of that notice. You cannot appeal before you have the notice. Tribunal pages on GOV.UK are the link. This site does not pull hearing lists or case files.

## What ChatLegal will and will not do

ChatLegal can point at the hardship route and at mandatory reconsideration as GOV.UK describes them. It will not write the reconsideration request. It will not say to wait, or to let the month pass. It will not predict a Department or tribunal outcome. It will not suggest withholding information from the Department.
